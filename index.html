1. Outside (The Stimulus Shift)

The neurobiology: The transition from noxious mechanical/thermal inputs to non-noxious low-threshold mechanical inputs. Old map (Neuralgia): Heavy boots stomping on the porch — true threats. New map (Allodynia): A leaf drifting onto the driveway or a light knock at the door (Aβ fiber activation via hair movement or light bedding touch).

2. Gate (Peripheral Afferents: Aδ & C-Fibers vs. Aβ-Fibers)

The left greater/lesser occipital nerves (C2/C3) suffer peripheral nerve injury. The damaged, thinly myelinated Aδ (fast, sharp pain) and unmyelinated C-fibers (slow, burning pain) develop altered sodium channel expression, causing spontaneous, ectopic pacemaking. Crucially, the Aβ fibers (large, heavily myelinated, normally handling light touch and vibration) are structurally intact but their signal is about to be misrouted.

Metaphor: The main security sensors (Aδ and C-fibers) are stripped, frayed, and constantly sparking false alarms. Meanwhile, the standard guest-buzzer (Aβ) is functioning normally, but it's wired into a shared electrical panel that is completely molten from the constant sparks.

3. Driveway (The Central Synapse & Neuropathic Modulation)

The presynaptic terminals in the spinal trigeminal nucleus / dorsal horn. Chronic barrage from the C-fibers releases glutamate and substance P, causing persistent depolarization of post-synaptic wide-dynamic-range (WDR) neurons via NMDA receptor activation. Her neuropathic medication (e.g., gabapentinoids) targets the α₂δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels here, reducing this excitatory neurotransmitter release.

4. House (The Brain + Fazekas Grade II)

The thalamocortical loops and descending pain modulatory systems (periaqueductal gray, rostral ventromedial medulla). At 82, Fazekas Grade II indicates moderate white matter hyperintensities — chronic microvascular ischemic damage. This directly degrades the subcortical structural integrity of her descending inhibitory pathways (which normally send serotonin and norepinephrine down to ground/gate the spinal cord).

5. Intercom / Feedback Loop (Central Sensitization + Panadol Extra)

Due to central sensitization, the Aβ mechanoreceptors — which normally terminate deep in Laminae III–V and never provoke a pain response — now either structurally sprout into Lamina II or experience functional cross-talk due to the loss of local GABAergic/glycinergic inhibitory interneurons. The brain now reads light touch (Aβ) as intense pain.

  • Panadol (Acetaminophen): Acts centrally to inhibit COX enzymes and modulate the endogenous cannabinoid/vanilloid systems, trying to lower the central amplification.
  • Caffeine: Blocks adenosine A₁ and A₂A receptors to enhance analgesia via vasoconstriction. However, because adenosine is a CNS depressant/inhibitory neuromodulator, blocking it keeps the central nodes in a high-gain, hyper-vigilant state.

Fazekas II & the descending brake line Response to: "Can't Fazekas II affect descending fibers in the pathways of interest?"

Yes, absolutely. In fact, it is highly probable that her Fazekas Grade II changes are a primary structural driver of her central sensitization and allodynia.

The Anatomy of the "Brake Line"

The descending pain modulatory system does not originate in the brainstem; it is driven by higher cortical nodes. The anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), and insula must send top-down projections down to the periaqueductal gray (PAG) and the rostral ventromedial medulla (RVM). The RVM then sends noradrenergic and serotonergic fibers down to the trigeminocervical complex (TCC) to hyperpolarize the dorsal horn and "close the gate."

To get from the prefrontal and cingulate cortices down to the brainstem, these descending axons must traverse the deep and periventricular white matter.

[Prefrontal / Cingulate Cortex] │ ▼ ◄─── [Fazekas II Ischemic Lesions Intercept Here] [Deep White Matter] │ ▼ [Periaqueductal Gray (PAG)] │ ▼ [Rostral Ventromedial Medulla (RVM)] │ ▼ (Serotonin / Norepinephrine Brake) [Trigeminocervical Complex / C2-C3] ──> Block Aβ Crossover

Fazekas Grade II as Structural Disconnection

Fazekas Grade II indicates moderate, confluent white matter hyperintensities. Pathologically, this represents chronic microvascular ischemia, punctate axonal loss, and demyelination. When these ischemic lesions accumulate in the deep subcortical tracks, they cause a structural disconnection syndrome. The cortical commands telling the brainstem to suppress pain are physically short-circuiting. The brain has essentially lost its structural "brake line."

Why This Directs the Aβ Hijacking (Allodynia)

In a healthy nervous system, tonic (baseline) descending inhibition continuously dampens wide-dynamic-range (WDR) neurons in the TCC. This keeps the threshold high, ensuring that low-threshold mechanoreceptor (Aβ) signals from a light touch or a hairbrush are read strictly as "touch."

Because her Fazekas II changes have frayed these descending inhibitory tracts: loss of gating leaves the TCC wide open; subthreshold depolarization means the baseline electrical potential of WDR neurons sits perilously close to firing threshold; the allodynia crossover follows because without the descending monoaminergic brake, innocuous Aβ inputs easily push sensitized WDR neurons over the edge.

The Clinical Insight: Why the Garden Worked

When she is at rest, she is relying on her passive, tonic descending inhibitory system — which is structurally broken by the Fazekas II lesions. The system cannot clear the signal on its own, so the pain spikes to a 7.

When she goes into the garden, the massive flood of multi-sensory data forces active, phasic cortical recruitment. This high-demand environmental engagement bypasses the damaged, passive white matter tracks by forcefully driving alternative, intact cortical networks to demand brainstem modulation. She essentially forces her brain to execute a manual override because the automatic brakes are frayed.

Why structural disease (Vₐₛ) fails the pattern

If she had been suffering from standard atherosclerotic coronary artery disease, a 20-mile boda boda ride across rough terrain, followed by walking out into a sugarcane plantation to supervise workers, would have driven her myocardial oxygen demand through the roof. She would have experienced severe exertional angina, dyspnea, or ischemia on the bike or while walking. Instead, she was perfectly capable of handling extreme physical stress.

Why functional vascular spasm (Vₐf) wins the differential

Prinzmetal variant angina is defined by transient, severe coronary artery spasms exclusively at rest, unrelated to exertional demand. The "spasms" she felt in her back, neck, and arm are classic dermatomal distributions of referred visceral cardiac pain, easily misattributed to musculoskeletal tracking (S). The intensity makes a stoic matriarch cry; the sudden drop in cardiac output triggers a primal, localized autonomic panic attack. Normal serum calcium further stripped away the skeletal-muscular hypothesis.

The lethal mechanism

In early May, she didn't die from the physical exertion of the journey. She died from the acute emotional altercation. Emotional stress triggers a massive, sudden surge of catecholamines (epinephrine and norepinephrine). In a patient with a hyper-reactive coronary vascular segment (Vₐf), this sympathetic storm acts as a massive alpha-1 adrenergic trigger. The vessel didn't just spasm and release; it clamped shut completely, causing acute transmural myocardial infarction or instantly triggering fatal ventricular arrhythmia (VF).
